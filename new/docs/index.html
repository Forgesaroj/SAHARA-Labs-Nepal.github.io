02 / SAHARA CORE

An operating core for accounting-led businesses.

Product direction: web-based accounting, inventory, and operating control with professional speed and explainable records.

Problems it is intended to address

  • Financial and stock data spread across disconnected tools
  • Slow voucher entry and reporting for experienced operators
  • Weak responsibility, approval, and correction trails
  • Limited visibility across companies, locations, or functions
  • Local accounting needs embedded as brittle exceptions

Core capability areas

  • Ledgers, vouchers, bill-wise records, banking, and reconciliation
  • Purchasing, sales, inventory movement, valuation, and counts
  • Tax workflows, fiscal periods, numbering, and document controls
  • Roles, company boundaries, approvals, audit history, and reports
  • Localization through explicit country and configuration layers
STRONGER FIT WHEN

Accounting accuracy, inventory movement, operator speed, and owner visibility must work as one controlled system.

REQUIRES DISCOVERY WHEN

Your workflow depends on specialized regulation, mature third-party integrations, unusual costing, or immediate production deployment.

03 / SAHARA QR DINE

One connected hospitality service flow.

Product direction: reduce handoff gaps between guest intent, kitchen execution, table service, payment, stock, and management visibility.
GUEST ORDER→KITCHEN→SERVICE→SETTLEMENT→INSIGHT

Evaluation questions

  • Where are orders delayed, repeated, or misunderstood?
  • How are modifications, availability, and guest requests communicated?
  • How do kitchen, service, and cashier teams see responsibility?
  • How are settlements and stock effects reconciled?

Useful pilot boundary

  • One venue or service area
  • A limited menu and defined service period
  • Named kitchen, service, and cashier participants
  • Baseline order, fulfilment, error, and settlement measures

06 / PILOT FRAMEWORK

Keep the first commitment bounded.

A pilot is a controlled learning engagement, not an implied production release. Its purpose is to produce decision-quality evidence.
IN SCOPE

One valuable workflow

Defined users, representative data, agreed environment, named ownership, and specific scenarios.

TIMEBOX

A fixed review window

Milestones for preparation, configuration, use, issue review, measurement, and the final decision.

CONTROLS

Explicit risk boundaries

Access, data handling, backups, support route, change authority, fallback, and exit conditions.

OUTCOME

A written decision

Stop, iterate, extend, or prepare rollout based on the agreed evidence—not enthusiasm alone.

A pilot brief should state

business problem · participants · process boundary · data classification · environments · responsibilities · acceptance criteria · timeline · risks · support route · exit plan

08 / IMPLEMENTATION

Move from evidence to controlled adoption.

If a pilot supports expansion, the rollout plan should remain phased, reversible, and clear about who owns each decision.
01

Blueprint

Confirmed process, roles, configuration, data, integration, reporting, and control requirements.

02

Prepare

Environment, configuration, access, migration rules, test cases, training, and support readiness.

03

Validate

Reconciliation, scenario tests, permission checks, user acceptance, recovery, and go/no-go review.

04

Adopt

Phased cutover, monitored use, issue ownership, fallback protection, and outcome review.

Migration principles

  • Profile and reconcile source data before transformation
  • Preserve source references and migration evidence
  • Use dry runs and signed acceptance checks
  • Limit access to necessary people and environments
  • Define cutover, fallback, and retention decisions
  • Reconcile opening positions and critical reports

09 / TRUST & DEPLOYMENT

Requirements to verify, not assumptions to make.

The following areas guide product architecture and discovery. Exact controls, responsibilities, availability, and contractual commitments must be confirmed for a specific engagement.

Identity & access

Roles, organizational boundaries, trusted authorization, session behavior, and administrative responsibility.

Data protection

Classification, collection, storage, transit, retention, deletion, export, and responsible access.

Integrity & audit

Atomic operations, correction paths, actor and time history, reconciliation, and explainable state changes.

Resilience

Backups, recovery objectives, monitoring, incident routes, retries, maintenance, and fallback procedures.

Deployment

Cloud and locally managed models are part of product direction; feasibility depends on the specific scope.

Assurance

No certification or country-wide compliance should be assumed unless SAHARA provides current, verifiable evidence.

Buyer responsibility

Your organization should involve its legal, privacy, security, accounting, and regulatory reviewers when their requirements apply. Product documentation is not a substitute for their approval.

11 / FAQ & STATUS

Current answers for prospective buyers.

Are SAHARA products generally available?

No general-availability claim is made here. The products are under active development. Contact SAHARA LABS to discuss current scope and whether a structured evaluation or pilot is appropriate.

Is pricing published?

Not in this documentation. Any commercial structure would depend on verified scope, deployment, implementation, support, risk, and product maturity. Request written terms before making a commitment.

Which countries and regulations are supported?

The architecture is intended to support explicit localization, beginning with Nepal-informed workflows. Country, tax, privacy, industry, and accounting requirements must be evaluated individually; do not infer legal compliance from design direction.

Can SAHARA integrate with our existing systems?

Integration is part of the engineering direction, but feasibility depends on interfaces, data quality, security, transaction ownership, operational risk, and the maturity of the required capability.

What happens if a pilot does not meet the criteria?

The correct outcome may be to stop, revise the problem, adjust the product, or postpone adoption. A bounded pilot should include export, fallback, and exit expectations before it starts.

Where can we follow public engineering work?

Visit the SAHARA LABS GitHub organization. Public repositories do not, by themselves, prove that a capability is released or supported.
